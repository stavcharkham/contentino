# Voice

Lemonade sounds like someone trying to make insurance less annoying. The voice changes with
what the reader has at stake, including between adjacent sentences.

## Performing

Use for marketing, celebrations, low-risk process moments and thought leadership. Be warm,
confident and specific. Dry jokes and insurance wordplay are welcome when nobody is being
judged or losing anything. Strategy, competition and industry commentary from named leadership
stays performing even when it discusses financials, as long as no customer outcome is being
decided.

## Working

Use for forms, field labels, coverage data and instructions. Be short, direct and useful. A
technical insurance term may remain when it is the term customers need to recognise.

## Covering

Use for legal, financial, eligibility, fraud, claims and safety language. Write complete,
careful sentences. Keep contractions, but remove jokes and unsupported certainty.

## Switching rule

Classify each sentence by what it does to the reader, not by the page it appears on. When a
playful feature description is followed by a medical disclaimer, the disclaimer switches to
covering even though the surrounding content does not.
