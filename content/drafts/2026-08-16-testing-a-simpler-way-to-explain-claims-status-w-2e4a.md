# Testing a Simpler Way to Explain Claims Status, With 200 Invited Renters Customers in New York

Starting 1 September 2026, we're running a four-week test with 200 invited renters-insurance customers in New York. This is an invitation-only test, not a general launch, and we haven't decided whether it will become a permanent feature.

Here's what's actually changing: the way we explain the current status of a claim and the next step to take. That's it. Coverage, claim decisions, payment timing and eligibility all stay exactly the same for these customers and for everyone else.

We're calling this a simpler claims-status view. It isn't a new claims experience, a rollout or a new feature. It's a small, invited group seeing clearer wording about where their claim stands and what to do next.

## Why we're doing this

When you file a claim, knowing your next step shouldn't require a phone call. So we're testing new wording with the goal of making that next step obvious. We'll be watching two things during the test: whether invited customers can identify their next step without contacting support, and whether the new wording reduces repeat questions about status.

A reduction in repeat questions is a goal we're measuring, not a result we're promising. We're not saying this speeds up claim decisions or payments, and it doesn't affect the outcome of any claim. It also doesn't promise fewer support contacts. It's meant to make the next step clear, not to make a claim look simpler than it is.

## Why now

The test runs from 1 September 2026 through the end of September 2026. We're sharing this now, while the test is live, because it only applies to 200 invited customers in New York, and we want to be upfront about what is and isn't changing while it's happening.

## What we'll learn, and what we won't claim yet

The test will tell us what customers understand about their claim status and next steps. It won't prove the idea works before the results exist. We haven't started the test yet, so we have no results or early signals to share, and we won't be reporting any until the test is complete and we've had a chance to review what we learned.

As Maya Chen, Claims Product Lead, put it: "If we write about it, the useful story is that we are testing clearer explanations of status and next steps with a small invited group. The test will tell us what customers understand. It will not prove the idea before the results exist."

If you're one of the 200 invited customers, nothing about your coverage or eligibility changes. If you have questions about the test, our support team is ready to help.
