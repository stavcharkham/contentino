# We're testing a simpler claims-status view with 200 renters in New York

Starting 1 September 2026, we're inviting 200 renters-insurance customers in New York to try a simpler claims-status view. The test runs for four weeks.

Here's what we want to learn. First, whether people can look at the screen and identify their next step without asking anyone. Second, whether the new wording reduces the number of repeat questions customers bring to support.

That's the whole scope. This is a change to how claim status is presented, not a change to how claims are reviewed or decided. Taking part in the test doesn't affect the outcome of a claim, the timing of a decision, or the timing of a payment. Those are handled the same way for everyone.

We'll know more when the four weeks are up.

## Why now

The weekly claims product meeting on 12 August 2026 set the test parameters: 200 invited customers, New York renters insurance, a 1 September 2026 start, four weeks. Publishing before the invitations go out gives customers and support teams the same description of what the test is and what it isn't.

## What changed

A test of a simpler claims-status view moved from discussion to a dated plan with a defined audience, duration and two measures. Nothing about claims handling changed.

## What this test is not

We want to be clear about the boundaries of this test. It isn't a new claims experience: it's a simpler claims-status view. It doesn't speed up decisions or payments, and it doesn't affect claim outcomes. We're not promising fewer support contacts. Reducing repeat questions is something we're measuring, not a result we're announcing today. And since the test hasn't started, we don't have results to share yet. This test is limited to the 200 invited New York renters-insurance customers. It isn't a preview of a wider rollout.

Source: Weekly claims product meeting, 12 August 2026.
