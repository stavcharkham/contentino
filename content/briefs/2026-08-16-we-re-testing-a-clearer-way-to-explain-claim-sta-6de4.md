# We're testing a clearer way to explain claim status

We're running a small test of a simpler claims-status view. Starting 1 September 2026, we're inviting 200 renters-insurance customers in New York to try it for four weeks.

Here's what the test does: it changes how we explain your existing claim status and your next step. That's it. It doesn't change your coverage, how claim decisions get made, when payments arrive, or who's eligible for what. The same claim, the same decision, the same timing, explained more clearly.

We're not trying to make a claim look simpler than it is. Some claims are complicated, and pretending otherwise wouldn't help anyone. What we want is for the next step to be obvious: what's happening now, and what happens next.

During the four weeks, we'll be measuring two things. First, whether invited customers can identify their next step without contacting support. Second, whether the new wording reduces repeat questions about status.

This is invitation-only. It's a test, not a general launch, and the test is what tells us whether the clearer wording actually lands.

## Why now

The test window opens 1 September 2026 and runs four weeks, so the announcement needs to be live before invited customers see an unfamiliar view in their account. Publishing during the window also sets expectations for everyone who isn't invited: this is 200 customers in New York, not a product change.

## What changed

Only the explanation layer changed. The claims-status view now describes existing claim status and next step in simpler language. Coverage, claim decisions, payment timing and eligibility are unchanged by this test.

## Purpose

- Serves: Comms and content teams drafting the public post; the claims product team that owns the test; support leads who'll field questions from customers who weren't invited; legal and compliance reviewers checking that the post doesn't overstate what the test does.
- Job: Explain a limited, invitation-only test of clearer claims-status wording without implying any change to coverage, claim decisions, payment timing or eligibility, and without promising an outcome the test is designed to measure.
- Metric: Two measures carried from the brief: whether invited customers can identify their next step without contacting support, and whether the new wording reduces repeat questions about status. For the post itself: no correction needed for overstating scope, and no support escalations from non-invited customers who thought their claims process changed.
- Shelf life: Live through the four-week window that starts 1 September 2026. Goes stale at the end of the test. Refresh triggers: the test ends and results are available; the invited group, geography or product line changes; the test is extended, expanded or cancelled; a decision is made about a general launch. Any claim about what customers understood needs a new source, because the transcript predates the results.

## Not saying

- Not that it speeds up claim decisions or payments. The notes explicitly prohibit this.
- Not that the view affects the outcome of a claim. It only changes how status and next step are explained.
- Not that support contacts will drop. Reducing repeat questions is a measurement, not a promise.
- Not a new claims experience. Call it a simpler claims-status view.
- Not a general launch. Invitation-only, 200 customers, New York, renters insurance.
- No claim about what customers understood. The test hasn't run yet.
