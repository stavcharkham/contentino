# We're testing a simpler claims-status view with 200 invited renters customers in New York

Starting 1 September 2026, we're running a four-week test of a simpler claims-status view with 200 invited renters-insurance customers in New York. Only one thing changes for those customers: the way we explain the current status of a claim and the next step to take. Coverage, claim decisions, payment timing and eligibility all stay exactly the same.

The goal is to make the next step clear, not to make a claim look simpler than it is. We'll be watching two things: whether invited customers can identify their next step without contacting support, and whether the new wording reduces repeat questions about status. A reduction is a goal, not a promised outcome.

This is an invitation-only test, not a general launch. We haven't decided whether it will become a permanent feature. The test will tell us what customers understand. It won't prove the idea before the results exist.

## Why now

The test window is fixed: 1 September 2026 through the end of September 2026. Anything published needs to reach readers before the four weeks end, and any framing has to survive Legal's boundaries on scope, since "invitation-only test" is the whole point.

## What changed

The claims product team agreed on 12 August 2026 to test clearer wording for claim status and next steps with a small invited group. Legal set the naming and the limits in the same meeting: it's a simpler claims-status view, not a new claims experience, and no external copy may say it speeds up decisions or payments, affects claim outcomes, or promises fewer support contacts.

## Purpose

- Serves: Content and brand writers drafting the external announcement, plus the Claims Product and Legal reviewers who'll sign it off. Support Operations can also use it to answer questions from invited customers about what the test is.
- Job: Give the writer a sourced, publishable frame for a small invitation-only test, with the exact naming Legal approved and the five claims that can't be made, so the draft reaches review with its boundaries already carried forward.
- Metric: The external draft clears Legal review without a scope correction on the first pass: correct name used, no speed or outcome claim, no support-contact promise, test framed as invitation-only with no permanence implied.
- Shelf life: Stale after the test ends in late September 2026. Refresh triggers: results arrive and need their own sourced brief; the test start date, customer count, state or duration changes; Legal revises the approved naming; or a decision is made about whether the view becomes permanent.

## Not saying

- Don't call it a new claims experience. Legal ruled that phrase is broader than what's changing. Call it a simpler claims-status view.
- Don't say it speeds up claim decisions or payments. Payment timing does not change.
- Don't imply the view affects the outcome of a claim.
- Don't promise fewer support contacts. Reducing repeat questions is a goal being measured, not a result.
- Don't describe this as a launch, a rollout or a new feature. It's an invitation-only test with 200 customers, and no decision has been made about making it permanent.
- Don't say coverage or eligibility changes for anyone. They don't.
- Don't report results or early signals. The test hasn't started, and the transcript contains no data.
