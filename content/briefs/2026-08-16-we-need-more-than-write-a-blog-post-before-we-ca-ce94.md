# We need more than "write a blog post" before we can brief this

The request that came through Slack was three words: "write a blog post." That's not enough to build a brief on, and we'd rather say so now than guess and hand you something that reads like it was written about Lemonade rather than for a reader. There's no topic, no audience, no source material and no approved claims attached to the request. Anything we drafted from here would be invented, and invented copy is exactly what our compliance rules block: unsupported claims about how we judge a customer, quotes without a traceable source, product promises nobody has approved. So this brief does the only honest job available to it. It records what was asked, records what's missing, and lists what we need back before drafting starts. Send us four things and we can turn this around properly: the topic and the one thing a reader should take away, who that reader is and what they have at stake, the source material or approved brief the claims come from, and any disclaimers the source requires us to carry forward. If the post touches price, eligibility, coverage, claims, fraud or data use, tell us that too, because it changes who reviews it and how carefully we write it. Give us a topic and a source, and we'll give you a blog post.

## Why now

The request arrived in Slack on its own, with no attached brief, topic or source. Nothing else in the transcript narrows it. Drafting now would mean inventing the subject matter, which is the fastest route to a piece that can't clear review.

## What changed

A request came in. That's the only fact on the record. No topic, audience, source material or approved claim set changed hands with it.

## Purpose

- Serves: The content or marketing team member who sent the request, plus whoever would have reviewed the draft. It gives both a single place to see what's still needed.
- Job: Stop an unsourced draft from entering the pipeline, and turn a three-word request into a specific list of inputs the requester can supply in one reply.
- Metric: The requester comes back with the topic, the reader, the source material and any required disclaimers, so drafting can start without invented claims.
- Shelf life: Stale as soon as the requester replies. Any of these triggers a refresh: a topic and audience arrive, source material or an approved brief is attached, or the request is withdrawn. If nothing comes back within a week, close it out.

## Not saying

- We're not saying the blog post is a bad idea. We're saying we can't tell yet what it's about.
- We're not saying anything about Lemonade's pricing, coverage, eligibility, claims handling or data use. None of that appeared in the request, so none of it appears here.
- We're not proposing a topic on the requester's behalf. Picking the subject is their call, not ours.
- We're not treating this as an approved brief. It's an intake note that needs a reply.
