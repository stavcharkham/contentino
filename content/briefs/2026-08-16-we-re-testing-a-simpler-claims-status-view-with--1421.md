# We're testing a simpler claims-status view with 200 invited customers

Starting 1 September 2026, we're testing a simpler claims-status view with 200 invited renters-insurance customers in New York. The test runs for four weeks. What changes is only the way an existing claim's status and next step are explained. It does not change coverage, claim decisions, payment timing or who is eligible. The goal is to make the next step clear, not to make a claim look simpler than it is. We're measuring two things: whether invited customers can identify their next step without contacting support, and whether the new wording reduces repeat questions about status. A reduction is a goal, not a promised outcome. This is an invitation-only test, not a general launch, and we haven't decided whether it will become a permanent feature. The test will tell us what customers understand. It won't prove the idea before the results exist.

## Why now

The test window is fixed and narrow: it opens 1 September 2026 and closes four weeks later. Any external writing has to land before or during that window while the framing is still accurate, and before results exist that would change what we can say. Legal set the boundaries in the 12 August 2026 claims product meeting, so the approved language is available now.

## What changed

The claims product team confirmed the scope, dates and measures for the test, and Legal ruled on how it can be described outside the team. The phrase "new claims experience" was rejected as broader than what's changing; the approved description is "a simpler claims-status view." Before this meeting there was no agreed external framing.

## Purpose

- Serves: The content and communications team writing anything external about the test, plus support operations and customer experience leads who'll be asked what's changing. Reviewers can use it to check that a draft carries the boundaries Legal set on 12 August 2026.
- Job: Give writers one sourced description of the test, with the approved name, the fixed scope and dates, the two measures, and the claims that are off-limits, so nothing published overstates an invitation-only test.
- Metric: Any external draft about this test passes review without a correction to scope or naming: it uses "simpler claims-status view," states the invited-only 200-customer scope, and makes no claim about decision speed, payment timing or reduced support contacts.
- Shelf life: Accurate through the four-week test window that ends around 29 September 2026. It goes stale the moment results exist, since the current framing rests on there being nothing yet to report. Refresh if the test dates move, if the invited group or state changes, if the team decides whether to make the view permanent, or if Legal revises the approved description.

## Not saying

- Not saying this speeds up claim decisions or payments. Legal ruled that out explicitly.
- Not saying the view affects the outcome of a claim.
- Not promising fewer support contacts. A reduction in repeat questions is a goal, not a promised outcome.
- Not calling this a new claims experience, a launch, or a feature that's here to stay. It's an invitation-only test, and no decision has been made about making it permanent.
- Not saying anything about what customers understood or preferred. Those results don't exist yet.
- Not describing eligibility, coverage or payment timing as changed, because they aren't.
