# Q2 2026 earnings call: brief for a blog post we can't write yet

A single Slack message asked for a blog post based on the Lemonade Q2 2026 earnings call transcript on fool.com. That's the entire input. The link text is in the transcript, but the transcript contains none of the call's contents: no growth figures, no loss ratio, no in-force premium number, no executive commentary, no forward guidance.

Earnings-call content is the highest-stakes category this profile handles. It's an attributed public-company claim about price, growth and performance, which means every number and every quoted sentence has to be traceable to the source document and carried forward with the source's own qualifiers intact. We can't paraphrase from memory, and we can't reconstruct what a CEO probably said. Under compliance, presenting an attributed quote or factual public claim without a traceable source is a blocking condition, not a style note.

So this brief does the only honest job available: it names what was asked, marks the gap, and specifies exactly what has to be pulled from the transcript before a draft exists. Once someone attaches the actual transcript text or a reviewed extract, the blog is writable in the covering register: complete sentences, no jokes, no unsupported certainty, and every metric matched to how the company itself framed it, including the period it covers and any non-GAAP labelling.

What we need, in order: the reported metrics and their exact wording; any forward-looking statements plus the safe-harbour language attached to them; direct quotes with speaker and role; and confirmation from Investor Relations or Legal on which of those we're cleared to repeat outside the filing.

## Why now

The request arrived in Slack with a link and no extracted content, and earnings copy has a short window where it's still news. Naming the missing inputs now is faster than reviewing a draft built on guesses.

## What changed

Nothing in the source changed. What changed is the request: a blog post was asked for on a topic where this profile can't generate a single factual sentence from what's in the transcript. That moves the piece from drafting to sourcing.

- Serves: The content lead who received the Slack request, plus the writer who'd otherwise start drafting, and the Investor Relations and Legal reviewers who'd have to catch invented figures downstream.
